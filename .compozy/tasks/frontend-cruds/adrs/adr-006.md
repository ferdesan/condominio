# ADR-006: Soft-Deleted Records Remain Reachable and Restorable

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-09-12

## Context

Deletion in all four modules is logical, not physical. A deleted record disappears from listings and direct reads, but persists, and every module exposes a restore route. Listings accept a flag that includes deleted records in the result. Restoration is governed by the update permission rather than the delete permission, so a role able to edit a record can also restore one.

Some deletions are already blocked by the backend when dependent data exists: a condominium with units, a unit with active residents or open charges. Deletions that are permitted are therefore precisely the ones affecting records with no dependents — where an accidental deletion is most plausible, because nothing warns the operator.

Market research found that audit trails are an advertised feature in this sector and that data-protection practice for resident records favors preserving history over destroying it.

## Decision

Each of the four listings offers a control to include deleted records, and rows shown that way are visually distinguished and offer a restore action, gated on the same permission the backend requires.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Deleted records are invisible

- **Description**: The interface never shows deleted records and never restores.
- **Pros**: Fewer states per screen; simpler listing logic; no ambiguity about whether a row is live.
- **Cons**: An accidental deletion becomes unrecoverable through the product, so recovery means a support request and direct database access.
- **Why rejected**: The backend already retains the data and exposes restoration; hiding that converts a recoverable mistake into an incident.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Accidental deletion is self-service to undo.
- The restore endpoints, present in all four modules, are reachable.
- Retaining rather than destroying resident records aligns with the data-protection posture the sector expects.

### Negative

- Each listing carries an additional state, and rows must communicate clearly whether they are live or deleted.
- A deleted record reappearing in a filtered listing can confuse an operator who does not notice the filter is active.

### Risks

- **Risk**: Restoring a unit or resident may re-trigger derived state, such as a unit's occupancy status recomputing. **Mitigation**: Refresh affected listings after restoration rather than patching state locally.
- **Risk**: A deleted record may be restored into a state that now conflicts — for instance a unit number reused in the interim. **Mitigation**: Surface the server's conflict message; the uniqueness checks that guard creation also consider soft-deleted records in the condominium case.

## Implementation Notes

Deletion returns 204 with no body. A deletion blocked by dependents returns 409 with a message naming the blocking relationship, and those messages are specific enough to show directly.

Uniqueness validation for condominium documents deliberately searches deleted records too, so creating a condominium whose document matches a deleted one conflicts rather than succeeding. The interface should present restoration as the remedy in that case.
