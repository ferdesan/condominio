# ADR-003: Closing Becomes One Transaction, and a Re-Close Replaces Its Movements

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-09-18

## Context

`ClosingService.close` writes exactly one row today, through
`this.closings.update(...)` or `.create(...)` — the `BaseRepository`
(`closing.service.ts:159-161`). With [ADR-002](adr-002.md) it writes that row
plus every movement of the month, and the two must land together: a closing whose
totals were saved and whose movements were not is a document that claims a number
it cannot show.

Two facts in the codebase decide how:

**`BaseRepository` cannot participate in a transaction.** Its `repository` getter
resolves `AppDataSource.getRepository(...)` — the DataSource's global repository,
not the transaction's `EntityManager` (`base.repository.ts:37-40`). Every write
method routes through it, and there is no seam to inject a manager. That is
exactly why all five existing transactions in the backend call `manager.create` /
`manager.save` directly and bypass the repositories.

**`update` merges; it never removes.** `base.repository.ts:89-97` applies the
patch over the row. Nothing in `close` today deletes anything, and nothing needs
to — the previous `breakdown` is simply overwritten by the new one. Movements in a
separate table have no such luck: a second close would add a second set alongside
the first, and the month would carry two versions of itself.

The second close is not hypothetical. `close` accepts a row whose status is
`OPEN` — a month closed, reopened and closed again, which is the flow
`balancete-mensal` IT-293 already exercises.

## Decision

`close` runs its writes inside `AppDataSource.transaction`, using the
transaction's `manager` directly:

1. delete every `financial_closing_entries` row of this closing — unconditionally,
   whether or not one is expected;
2. insert the movements of the recomputed month, in chunks, as
   `charge.service.ts:156-161` already does for bulk charges;
3. insert or update the `financial_closings` row.

The delete comes first and is not conditional on `existing`. A guard that only
cleans "when there was a previous close" is a guard that trusts its own bookkeeping;
deleting what should not be there costs one statement and removes the whole class
of duplicate.

`this.audit.record(...)` stays **outside** the transaction, after it commits —
the placement `charge.service.ts:163-173` and `unit.service.ts:133-143` already
use. An audit write is fire-and-forget by design (`audit.service.ts:85-88`) and
must not be able to roll back a financial fact.

`reopen` does **not** delete the movements. They stay on the row, unread: while
the month is `OPEN` the screen computes live, exactly as the summary already
does. Deleting on reopen would destroy the previous document for a month that may
simply be reopened and closed again unchanged.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Keep the repository calls, add the entries after

- **Description**: write the closing as today, then write the movements.
- **Pros**: the smallest diff; `close` keeps its shape.
- **Cons**: two independent writes. A failure between them leaves totals without
  movements, and the screen shows a document missing its body with nothing to say
  why.
- **Why rejected**: the invariant being bought is that the list and the totals are
  one document. Two writes cannot buy it.

### Alternative 2: Give `BaseRepository` an optional `manager`

- **Description**: thread an `EntityManager` through the repository's write
  methods so `close` keeps using them.
- **Pros**: transactions everywhere, without bypassing the repositories.
- **Cons**: changes the shared base class of every module for one caller, and
  the five existing transactions would still not use it.
- **Why rejected**: out of proportion. Worth revisiting when a second caller
  needs it.

### Alternative 3: Delete the movements when the month is reopened

- **Description**: `reopen` clears the frozen rows.
- **Pros**: no stale rows on an open month; less storage.
- **Cons**: throws away the document of a month that may be closed again
  untouched, and makes reopening destructive when it is supposed to be reversible
  and recorded.
- **Why rejected**: reopening already carries the weight of undoing a rendered
  account; it should not also erase it.

## Consequences

### Positive

- A closed month is written whole or not at all.
- Re-closing is structurally incapable of duplicating movements.
- The audit entry describes a commit that actually happened.

### Negative

- `close` is the first place in the financial module to mix a transaction with
  service-level logic, and the only one where the repository is deliberately
  bypassed.
- The recomputed statement is held in memory before writing. A month with
  thousands of movements builds a large array before the first insert.

### Risks

- The chunked insert (`{ chunk: 100 }`) is the pattern used by bulk charge
  generation and by unit generation; it has not been exercised at the size a
  large condominium's month reaches. The cases pin correctness, not throughput.

## Implementation Notes

`manager.create(Entity, row)` needs `tenantId` set explicitly on each row — the
repository is what normally injects it from the scope, and the manager does not.
Both existing bulk writes do this (`charge.service.ts:134`, `unit.service.ts:108`).
