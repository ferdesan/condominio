# ADR-005: Export and Print Move to the Detail Route

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-09-18

## Context

The section in `/financeiro` carries both exports today: a `@media print` sheet
keyed on `.print-document` / `.print-hide` (`index.css:201-250`, applied at
`closing-section.tsx:97` and `:108`) and a client-side CSV built from the payload
the section holds (`closing-csv.ts`).

Both were designed around a payload that is the summary. With the movements on a
route of their own, the section's payload stops being the whole document — and an
export that quietly ships less than the page next to it shows is the kind of
defect nobody notices until an assembly asks where a payment went.

## Decision

Printing and CSV move to `/financeiro/balancete/:mes`.

- The print sheet's markers move with them: the detail route's root carries
  `print-document`, its controls carry `print-hide`. The CSS block itself does
  not change — it names classes, not screens, which is what makes the move cheap.
- `buildClosingCsv` grows a second block: the summary it already emits, then the
  movements, under a header that names them. The file stays one file, because a
  rendered account is one document.
- The section keeps a link — "ver o documento completo" — and loses its two
  export buttons.

One place exports, and it exports everything it shows.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Both screens export

- **Description**: the section exports the summary; the detail route exports the
  full document.
- **Pros**: whoever is looking at the summary does not have to navigate to
  export it.
- **Cons**: two files, same subject, different contents, and — under
  `closingCsvFileName` — the **same name**. Two downloads called
  `balancete-2026-08.csv` that disagree is a support ticket waiting to be filed.
- **Why rejected**: the collision is not a detail; it is the whole problem.

### Alternative 2: Exports stay in the section

- **Description**: the detail route is for reading; exporting stays where it is.
- **Pros**: nothing already delivered changes.
- **Cons**: the exported document would omit the movements that the route beside
  it exists to show.
- **Why rejected**: it makes the export the least complete view of the three.

## Consequences

### Positive

- The exported file and the printed page are the same document as the screen,
  still by construction.
- The print sheet keeps working untouched, because it was written against class
  names rather than against a screen.

### Negative

- Exporting now costs a navigation from the summary.
- `closing-section.test.tsx` loses its export case (IT-314) and gains a case for
  the link. The test contract of the previous workflow changes as a consequence
  of this one — recorded in `_tests.md` rather than left to discovery.

### Risks

- The print sheet's only proof is structural: a case asserting the marker classes
  are where the CSS looks for them (`closing-section.test.tsx:230-247`). That case
  moves to the new screen, and the visual check — which `jsdom` cannot do —
  remains a human step, as `balancete-mensal` task_05 already records.

## Implementation Notes

`buildClosingCsv` keeps its signature over the statement and takes the movements
as a second argument rather than reading them from a wider object. The summary of
a month with no movements loaded is still a valid export, and a required second
argument would make that unrepresentable.
