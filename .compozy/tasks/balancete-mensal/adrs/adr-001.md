# ADR-001: Cash Basis for the Monthly Statement

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-09-18

## Context

`docs/balancete-mensal-plano.md` opens on the fact that the data supports two
readings of the same month and the system currently mixes them. `charge` carries
`reference_month` (what was billed for a month) and `payments` carry `paid_at`
(when money actually arrived). `expense` carries `competence` (when the cost was
incurred) and `paid_at` (when it was settled).

The existing aggregate `GET /financial/charges/summary` sums by
`reference_month` and reads `charge.paid_amount` as "received"
(`charge.repository.ts:76-82`), which is neither regime cleanly: the receipt
figure is attributed to the month the charge belongs to, not the month the money
came in. A statement built on that would not reconcile against a bank statement
in any month where a resident pays late — which, per the seed's own delinquency
model, is most months.

A condominium's monthly statement is read in an assembly, against a bank
statement, by people who did not write the software.

## Decision

The monthly statement is computed on a **cash basis**.

- Income for month M is `SUM(payment.amount)` over payments whose `paid_at`
  falls inside M.
- Expense for month M is `SUM(expense.amount)` over expenses with
  `status = 'PAID'` whose `paid_at` falls inside M.
- Delinquency is reported as an **auxiliary panel**, never as part of the
  result, and is frozen into the closing snapshot (ADR-005).

Charge-side fields — `reference_month`, `amount`, `interest`, `penalty`,
`discount` — do not enter the statement at all. Interest, penalty and discount
reach the statement only through the amount a resident actually paid, which is
what `payment.amount` already records.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Accrual basis (competence)

- **Description**: income from `charge.reference_month`, expense from
  `expense.competence`.
- **Pros**: shows the real cost of operating the building in the month it was
  incurred; matches how a budget is written.
- **Cons**: does not reconcile against a bank statement; and
  `ChargeService.applyLateFees` (`charge.service.ts:308-344`) mutates `penalty`
  and `interest` on already-overdue charges retroactively, so the billed figure
  for a past month legitimately changes after the fact.
- **Why rejected**: the product owner chose the reading the assembly can verify
  against the bank. The retroactive mutation would also mean a closed accrual
  month is only as frozen as the snapshot, with no guard able to explain the
  drift.

### Alternative 2: Both bases, side by side

- **Description**: the statement carries two columns and explains the delta.
- **Pros**: no reading is left out.
- **Cons**: doubles every query and forces the screen to teach accounting to its
  reader; the two columns disagree by design, and a document whose own two
  numbers disagree invites the question the statement exists to answer.
- **Why rejected**: scope and clarity. Accrual can be added later as a second
  view over the same closing record without changing what is stored.

## Consequences

### Positive

- The closing balance of a month is a number that can be compared to a bank
  balance, which is the only external check this product has.
- Retroactive charge mutation (`applyLateFees`, the overdue job) cannot alter a
  past statement, because no charge field feeds it.
- Partial payments need no special case: three payments against one charge are
  three cash movements in the months they happened.

### Negative

- The statement does not show the cost of operating the building in the month it
  was incurred. A December expense paid in January belongs to January here.
- `GET /financial/expenses/summary` — which sums by `competence` — answers a
  different question than the statement, and no screen consumes it today. It is
  left in place and unused rather than repurposed.

### Risks

- A reader who knows accounting may expect competence and read the wrong number.
  Mitigation: the screen names the regime in words, not only in a tooltip.
- `payment` rows are the sole source of income. A payment registered with a
  wrong `paid_at` moves money between months with no other record to contradict
  it. Mitigation: `paid_at` is required by `registerPaymentSchema` already, and
  the closed-month guard (ADR-003) refuses to move cash into a frozen month.

## Implementation Notes

Both sides sum from the same query that produces the per-category rows, and the
headline total is the sum of those rows in memory — never a second `SUM`. Two
independent sums over the same table drift the moment one of them grows a filter
the other does not.
